Concurrency check

Reading these charts

  • Each bar is the typical (median) speedup in bits for questions grouped by how hard the baseline found them. Each bit is a factor of two.
  • Solid bars are the three hardest groups that count, and they make up the credit. Light bars count but are not among the hardest. Dashed bars have too few questions, or sit below the 0.5 s floor, so they do not count.
  • The dashed line is the credit. It sits below the bars on purpose: it is the cautious number, not the typical one.
  • The "speedup by problem size" chart asks a different question: does the speedup grow as the problem gets harder? It shows the typical speedup at each size with a 90% range. Where the baseline ran out of time, its cost is taken as the time limit, so those questions can only understate the speedup. Hollow points are sizes the credit does not use (too many baseline time-outs, or costs under the 0.5 s floor). They are lower bounds or reference points, not measurements, and nothing is guessed beyond them or joined by a line.
  • A group can show a negative speedup: a tuned solver with a fixed setup cost is slower on trivial questions. That is why the floor exists, and it is shown, not hidden.
